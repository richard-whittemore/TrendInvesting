## Question

Do the Go target-weight modules (`internal/targetweight/momentum`,
`internal/targetweight/boost`) decide what the QuantConnect backtests
decided, fed the backtests' own inputs (ADR 0026 point 7, item 2;
`docs/live-trading/plan.md` section 6, item 2)?

## Thresholds

Fixed before any parity run and posted to #398 before any comparison
(issue comment of 2026-10-18, local commit ca1198e):

| Check | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| months | every planned month exported, no other |
| ranked order (`ranked-order`), cap marks (`cap-marks`) | exact: Go's `SelectBook` given QuantConnect's own scores, volatilities, sectors and risk-off flag |
| weights | absolute difference at most 1e-12, same inputs |
| scores, volatility | Go's `Score` recomputed from QuantConnect's adjusted closes, relative difference at most 1e-9 |
| risk-off | Go's `EvaluateTrend` from SPY's closes: flag and close exact, 200-day mean within 1e-9 relative |
| history | each sampled name's status (scored, or too short with the same close count) exact |
| Boost RSI (`boost-rsi`), means (`boost-sma`) | Go's indicators rebuilt from the first close: bit-exact, readiness included, every session |
| Boost state (`boost-state`) | latched state and SPY target exact, every session, carried on Go's own readings |
| differences | any difference not on ADR 0026 point 8's list fails; listed ones are reported with their size |

A check that compares nothing fails. "Bit-exact" is how the slice brief
read #324's "match every session" for Boost's indicators.

## Method

- **Export.** The Free plan keeps 10 KB of log and its object store cannot
  be written (a probe backtest, dc26a62971358f3c98fdc4d98cce20f2, was
  refused: "The current user does not have permission to write"), but an
  order tag holds at least 500 KB (probe 58c84f9c4a81407a8a97d323179d33b9)
  and `UpdateTag` works on an open order (probe
  a2cf60445834dc477771b6af71b4c962). So the logging templates
  (`momentum/parity_log.py`, `boost/parity_log.py`, additions only to
  `fresh_universe.py` and `template.py`, checked by a test) put each
  export into the tag of an order the strategy places anyway. With no open
  order they place a 1-share SPY limit order at 1% of the price and cancel
  it at once.
- **Momentum export, per month:** SPY's close, its 200-day mean and close
  count, the risk-off flag; every scored member in rank order with score,
  volatility, Morningstar sector and mark; the weights; the members too
  short to score with their close counts; and the adjusted closes, on the
  260 rows of the rebalance's history, of every pick, up to 5 names each
  capped, beyond the book, too short and scored across a missing close, and
  SPY. Numbers are written with `repr`, so they round-trip exactly.
- **Boost export:** every bar's close, RSI(2), 200-day and 5-day means
  (null until ready) and the state and target.
- **Comparison:** `cmd/parity` (`internal/parity`), tested first on
  synthetic exports: passes on matching ones, fails on each kind of
  alteration.
- Exports and full reports are market data and stay outside the
  repository, in `~/Desktop/Trend_Investing/data/parity/`:
  `<run>_orders.json`, `<run>_backtest.json`, `report_<run>.md`.

### What passed

- **The momentum rule.** Given QuantConnect's own scores, volatilities and
  sectors, Go ranked all 14,601 (1999-2015) and 15,229 (2016-2026) scored
  names in the same order: every rank compared, none attributed. Cap marks
  were compared exactly for 12,996 and 14,041 names and matched; the other
  1,605 and 1,188 were not compared but attributed to the no-sector names
  (769 and 533 of them the no-sector names themselves, 836 and 655 the
  marks after them). Weights were compared one by one only in the six
  months whose picked set the no-sector rule left alone, 25 weights in
  2015-11 and 127 in 2022-04, 2022-06, 2022-09, 2023-02 and 2026-06, all
  bit-identical (maximum difference 0); the other 850 and 751 targets were
  attributed, not compared. *(Corrected 2026-10-18: this bullet first said
  Go "gave each the same mark" and that Go's `BacktestWouldPick` matched
  QuantConnect's picks in all 61 months. The marks after a no-sector name
  were attributed, not compared, and the tool does not read
  `BacktestWouldPick`, so that sentence described a comparison it did not
  make. The counts are those of the "Tool v3 re-run" below; the first
  tool's weights counts are explained in the next sentence.)*
  The weights row's counts (875, 878) also include the targets of the
  months put down to the no-sector difference, which were not compared
  one by one.
- **Scores and volatility** recomputed from the closes: 1,050 and 1,083
  sampled names, every score and every volatility bit-identical.
- **Risk-off:** the flag and SPY's close matched in all 61 months, the
  200-day mean within 8.8e-16 relative.
- **Short and gapped histories:** all 1,183 and 1,223 sampled names had the
  same status and close count. No sampled scored name had a missing close
  in its window.
- **Boost's decisions:** the latched state and SPY target matched on every
  one of 7,166 sessions, on Go's own readings from the first close.

### What failed

1. **Differences, both momentum runs (#461).** QuantConnect's universe holds
   names with no Morningstar sector: 769 of 14,601 ranked names in the
   tuning months (5.3%), 533 of 15,229 held out (3.5%), mostly foreign ADRs
   (TSM, TEVA, BP, AMX, NVS, NOK, ASML, SAP, BIDU; JD, BABA, NTES) and recent
   listings (PLTR, SNOW, DASH, U, CARR). `defensive_book` picked them
   uncapped: 116 picks in the 30 tuning months, 97 in the 31 held out. Go
   leaves them out (`plan.md` section 4, "Sectors", "Chosen"), which changes
   the book from that rank down: in those months Go shares 12 to 24 of its
   25 picks with the backtest (25 in the six months above). The live rule is
   deliberate but is not on ADR 0026 point 8's list, so every such
   difference fails: 1,634 differences (769 names, 836 marks that follow
   from them, 29 months' weights) and 1,214 (533, 655, 26). No tie was
   reordered (the tuning months held one tie, which Go and QuantConnect
   ordered alike) and no zero volatility occurred.
2. **Boost RSI (#462).** Not bit-exact on 6,777 of 7,166 sessions; largest
   difference 4.26e-12 (6.1e-13 relative); readiness exact. LEAN computes
   indicators in C# `decimal`, the Go module in float64, so bit equality is
   out of reach of any float64 implementation. The nearest RSI(2) came to
   the entry threshold of 10 was 0.0027.
3. **Boost moving averages (#463).** Not bit-exact on 8,594 of 14,332
   readings; largest difference 5.68e-13 (1.3e-15 relative for the 200-day
   mean, 3.5e-16 for the 5-day); readiness exact. The nearest a close came
   to its 200-day mean was 1.6e-5 relative.

What changed in the tool:

- **Attribution is narrow.** Before, once any cause (a tie, a no-sector or
  zero-volatility name) appeared anywhere in a month's ranking, every later
  cap-mark mismatch and every weight mismatch of that month was put down to
  it, so a real Go defect could pass. Now a mark mismatch is attributed only
  at or after a cause QuantConnect's book held (marked P or C), and the
  weights only when the picked sets differ and such a cause exists; every
  other mark and weight is compared and a mismatch fails.
- **Attributed items are not counted as compared.** Each check has an
  `Attributed` count and column; `Compared` counts comparisons only, and a
  check with nothing compared still fails.
- **A listed difference must show in QuantConnect's data**: SPY's close
  count under 200, a missing close in the window, a volatility of exactly
  zero, no sector. If Go reports one that the data does not show, the
  relevant check fails.
- **Fail closed on the unexpected**: Go and QuantConnect must rank the same
  number of names (else `ranked-order` fails); a Go status with no backtest
  mark fails `export`; the decoder refuses a required field that is missing
  or null and an empty ticker; dates must be strictly increasing and the
  member count must equal ranked plus too short.

## Tool v4 re-run, 2026-10-18

The comparison was re-run on the kept exports, with no new QuantConnect
run, after the tool was changed a third time. **This change, like v3's,
was made after the earlier results had been seen**: review found that
v3's rule for putting a cap-mark or weight mismatch down to a listed cause
could still pass a real Go defect (a cap error after a no-sector pick, an
unrelated pick in a month with a tie inside the book, a capped
zero-volatility name taken as a cause). Rather than narrow that rule
again, it was removed. The change was made test first, on synthetic
exports; no threshold changed. The earlier reports are kept unchanged
(`report_FU_PARITY_tool_v1.md`, `report_FU_PARITY.md`,
`report_OOS_FU_PARITY.md`, `report_BX_PARITY.md` and the three
`*_tool_v3.md`); this run's are `report_FU_PARITY_tool_v4.md`,
`report_OOS_FU_PARITY_tool_v4.md` and `report_BX_PARITY_tool_v4.md`.

**The rule from v4.** The tool never puts a mismatch down to a cause. It
compares the momentum module's recomputation under the backtest's own
handling of each listed case and of a name with no sector
(`BacktestBook`: no-sector names ranked and left uncapped, ties kept in
QuantConnect's order, a zero volatility weighted as 1; `BacktestScore`: a
window over the closes a name has; `BacktestTrend`: SPY's mean over the
closes it has, risk-on under 200) with QuantConnect's export exactly:
ranked order, cap marks, picked set and weights, every month, with no
tolerance beyond the specification's. Separately, the differences check
lists every departure of the live rule (`SelectBook`, `Score`,
`EvaluateTrend`) from that backtest-compatible result, classified by
cause: the live rules are applied to the backtest-compatible book one at
a time (tie order by ID, a gap, a zero volatility, no sector), each change
is listed under the rule that made it, and the live book must then equal
the last step exactly, `BacktestWouldPick` included; anything else is
`unexplained`. Each cause must be on ADR 0026 point 8's list or the check
fails. The `Attributed` count is gone; `Compared` counts comparisons.

Other changes: the risk-off check also compares SPY's close count, and the
decoder refuses a null weight or close count and a missing `weights` or
`short` field.

One limit, stated rather than hidden: the export does not carry the order
the backtest's universe arrived in, which is how it broke ties, so
`BacktestBook` keeps tied names in QuantConnect's exported order. The
ranked-order check therefore proves the order of every untied pair, not
the order within a tie. The tuning months held one tie and the held-out
months none.

A second limit of the same kind: the gap rule is applied only to the
gapped names the export carries, which are the picks and at most five
sampled gapped names per month. A month with more gapped names than that
leaves the rest unchecked, so a live gap among them is not listed. No gap
occurred in any month of these runs.
